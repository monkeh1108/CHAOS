# CHAOS v0.36.4q — Foot Contact, Trips & Hurdles QA

**Build:** updated from the *exact* v0.36.4p standalone, reconstructed from the v0.36.4o full-source archive plus the v0.36.4p source patch. Build reproducibility was confirmed by matching the reconstructed v0.36.4p HTML byte-for-byte against the latest v0.36.4p file in the user's library before editing.

## Features implemented

1. **Foot/ankle geometry:** nearest swept relative approach from prior/current positions (cross-frame tunneling approximation), directional foot reach, vertical clearance, defender-grounded reach eligibility and minimum meaningful contact. Does not require a successful grip or tackle award.
2. **Stumbles and trips:** real low contacts affect velocity, balance and lean. A strong impact on an unstable runner can create a trip and stop the play; a light contact can cause a stumble, while a wide miss has no physical effect.
3. **Manual and automatic clearance:** Y uses timed `airState` height rather than a random shield. The existing mobile HURDLE UI is retained. An automatic small hop is possible against a downed player, but low hands can still contact feet near takeoff/landing. Full-sized jump clears higher than an auto hop.
4. **Airborne vulnerability:** real upright body hits still occur on a hurdler; loss of balance is amplified in the air, with procedural recoil. This is *groundwork*, not fully articulated airborne impact or a contested-catch collision solver.

## Human QA still needed

- Run straight into a safety diving at your feet at multiple speeds, then repeat while pressing Y early, late, and right at takeoff. Look for a stumble or trip on meaningful contact, but occasional successful escapes and clear hurdles.
- Sprint toward a downed defender and watch small automatic hops; a toe/landing ankle grab may still disrupt the stride.
- Try a head-on tackle by an upright defender while hurdling; the jump must *not* suppress upper-body collisions.
- Replay a safety blitz pickup by a running back to confirm angled hand fighting remains natural.

**Limits:** Contact geometry remains approximate and 2.5D for airborne movement, not continuous skeletal rigid-body simulation; tripping rates need live gameplay tuning. Detailed midair hit/landing animations and contested-catch ball-security behavior are not included yet. Passing automated tests does not prove every tackle looks correct in human gameplay.
