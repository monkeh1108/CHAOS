# CHAOS v0.36.4f — Calibration and approach-angle QA

## Scope
- Dive pose: longer forward-reaching arms in the 3D renderer and matching forward forearm/hand capsules in the anatomical observer; no modification to live tackle success logic.
- Glancing-contact lab preset: slightly reduced lateral offset, keeping the original body sizes rather than widening hitboxes.
- Eight deterministic approach options: west/rear, east/head-on, north/south 90° side contacts, northwest/northeast/southwest/southeast diagonals. Defensive orientation follows travel direction; relative speed and angle appear in the readout.
- All collision lab outputs are **detection only**. Neither an arm graze nor full contact automatically awards a tackle.

## Automated runs
- `python build_standalone.py --check`: PASS.
- `node qa_v36_4e_contact.js`: PASS, fast swept collision, misses, dimensions, recording disable/reset.
- `python qa_v36_4e_lab.py`: PASS, lab UI and live observer at desktop/mobile widths.
- `python qa_v36_4f_angles.py`: PASS, both 1120px and 390px widths. All eight directional setups and dive scenario produced contact; wide miss was clear; glancing setup yielded arm/hand contact.
- `python qa_v36_4c_endzone_choice.py`: PASS, kickoff return/touchback decision regression on desktop/mobile.

## Limitations
- The lab's scenarios are controlled geometric samples, not full live-game tackling physics. The existing tackle outcome solver remains in control.
- The arm-leading pose is procedural and simplified, and cannot establish NFL legality of a tackle on its own. Full tackle reactions, impact physics, injury/penalty modeling, and leg-wrap effects remain future work.
- Angle tests validate anatomical contact in each direction, not balanced tackle success rates at each angle.
- No new third-party libraries or assets were introduced.
