# CHAOS v0.36.4p — Escape Reaction QA

## Scope of the patch

v0.36.4o baseline retained. The main defect was in `releaseTackle`: every successful break unconditionally set `d.ground` to `fall`, scaled down defender velocity, and displayed `BREAK TACKLE`. This meant losing a grip was indistinguishable from falling down.

The new `src/escape_reactions.js` calculates physical instability from balance, relative separation/tangential velocity, actual spring force, grip quality, and committed diving posture. The standing defender can slip, turn, be dragged a short distance or stagger; a fall requires an ongoing dive or substantial physical imbalance. No random animation controls whether a grip is broken.

Active hold strain is fed to `AnimationPolicy` for observable leg drive, torso bracing and pull reactions **before** release. Body movement, tackle outcome and real attachment remain under engine physics. A modest stamina/balance/exertion cost accumulates over successive breaks, and fades with recovery. There is no hardcoded maximum number of breaks per play.

## Scope limitations / what still needs human testing

- The new strain is a **procedural visual reaction**, not complete planted-foot, skeletal or ragdoll physics. Arms and body postures are approximate at unusual angles; momentum and actual contact still come from the existing engine.
- Confirm during multiple real drives that failed standing arm tackles mostly leave defenders upright; diving defenders still go to ground; the runner visibly twists/drives before a release; tackle count remains plausible.
- The five-escape sequence is a deterministic simulation fixture, not a fully verified 70-yard gameplay recreation.
- The underlying tackle-escape model is still heuristic and needs player-stat and event-frequency calibration; multiple independent defenders may engage nearly simultaneously.
- No other ratings or gameplay modules changed except the existing escape policy's exertion multiplier, engine integration, and presentation policy.
