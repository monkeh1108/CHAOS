# CHAOS v0.36.4w — Rear Tackle Eligibility & Safety Run Recognition

## Reported failures
- Outside Stretch: stationary runner pushed ~25 yards by defenders behind him without being tackled.
- Kickoff: several defenders behind stationary returner collided and pushed without securing a tackle.
- Deep safeties often stayed turned away from the run until runner reached the first-down area.

## Verified reproduction / diagnosis
- A controlled scrimmage rear-contact fixture with three defenders and lingering blocking penalty reproduced 15.7+ yards of forward pushing in the predecessor, with no completed tackle by 2 seconds.
- Defensive tackle gating considered residual `blocked` status even when there was no longer a live blocker near the runner, so real physical contact could produce collision impulses while tackling was ineligible.
- A safety kept artificially facing away did not call run recognition during the 1.2-second fixture despite a confirmed ball carrier; the sole recognition condition required a direct cone-of-vision read or very short distance.

## Focused changes
- When a defender is physically touching a ball carrier, is not in a nearby live block, and has completed shed recovery, clear the stale block suppression so the normal contact/grip contest can proceed. Active blocks and legitimate recovery remain respected.
- Avoid committing fresh CPU low dives from close range against a nearly stationary runner; let controlled wraps compete normally. Dive-contact physics remains unchanged.
- Confirmed live handoff can trigger rating-delayed recognition from deep safety positions after an additional 0.16 seconds; no pre-handoff knowledge or instant turn. Existing turn/sprint physics remain intact.
- Added debug-only `debugSetDefenderState` for controlled fixtures, not player controls.

## Remaining limits
- This isolates the stuck-block and unrecognized-backfield conditions; user should still test naturally occurring 22-player kickoff and scrimmage pileups, which were not deterministically reproduced end-to-end.
- The test's forced line-of-sight posture is intentionally stricter than live gameplay. Actual safety reads continue to depend on rating, visuals and on-field geometry.
- No broad tuning of all blocking / safety attributes or contact UI changes.
