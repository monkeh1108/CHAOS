# Gridiron Lab v0.36.4 — Inside Zone Intelligence and browser pause performance

## Scope and design decisions

This update is limited to Inside Zone decision-making, its existing contact physics, and a separate low-resource *paused* rendering adjustment. Outside Stretch, Power, Counter, regular pass blocking, passing / catching, quarterback sacks, game clock, camera, and tackle rules were not broadly rewritten.

- **Assignment recognition at the snap:** Five interior offensive linemen identify aligned rushers and set primary gap opponents. Nearby linemen form a maximum two-man combo on an appropriate interior defender rather than all choosing the nearest man.
- **Secured vertical push:** A controlled velocity impulse through an existing hand-fighting/block contact increases displacement on won Inside Zone engagements, based on block quality, mass and secured leverage, including combo help. It never teleports defenders or opens a guaranteed gap.
- **Second-level support:** When the original threat has been beaten, blocked out of the relevant lane, or knocked down, the blocker may climb toward an unclaimed defender approaching the projected RB lane. Assignment retention is prioritized; nearby linebackers are scored by closing angle, estimated lane threat, rating and physical accessibility. Multiple linemen avoid taking the same available secondary defender.
- **Human perception and reaction delay:** New Inside Zone decisions only use a roughly forward 180° field of view. A blocker coming off a won engagement has an AWR-dependent reacquisition delay; even an elite player cannot instantly snap toward someone behind him. Defenders use their existing PRC-based run recognition and gap-fit assignments plus a nearby visible-blocker response, not perfect knowledge of the offensive play.
- **Performance:** A game left *paused* continues accepting input but renders a still field only roughly three times per second instead of 60. This reduces CPU/GPU contention with video playback; running gameplay is unaffected, and the code does not claim to cure all active-tab video buffering.

## Follow-up and known limitations

- **Animation interruption is a mandatory design requirement, not a feature claimed in v0.36.4.** The next animation upgrade should make pose transitions responsive to contact: shed a block → brush another opponent → brace/stumble/redirect/engage without a canned lock or teleport. Physics must own movement; animation should respond to it. Preserve action commitments only where physically meaningful (e.g. airborne dive), without animation-based steering locks.
- Human peripheral vision, communication, occlusion, and gaze shifts are currently simplified as a forward-facing cone. More complete perceptual models and player-specific recognition mistakes are future goals.
- This update models a simple combo assignment and reaction prototype, not a full NFL offensive playbook. Outside Zone reach blocks, pullers and advanced protections remain separate upcoming iterations.
- The performance adjustment reduces work **while paused**. Heavy rendering during actively playing a match can still compete with other browser workloads.
- Controller play, long-game balance, opponent AI adaptations under many fronts, and subjective visual realism still require user gameplay testing.
